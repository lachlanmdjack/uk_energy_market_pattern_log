## Cross Commodity Futures

### NBP Gas Futures

-   Gas futures for Q4 have exceeded 150p/Th, officially marking this week with the highest prices for the product in the last 2 years which ICE report (the start of the conflict only reached 152p/Th). There is minimal existing pressure that may force these prices down to pre-war levels or levels seen throughout June.

- EU gas storage injection has apparently slowed down over the last month, where in late June the year to date injection was above the 5-year minimum, whereas today we sit at the 5-year minimum. This brings winter tightness, where cold snaps (or arguably seasonally normal winter conditions) across the continent could rapidly draw gas down to levels that trigger high prices. Increased need on LNG imports from Henry Hub and other facilities may create bidding wars with Asia's JKM as was seen in 2021 and 2022, pushing prices up if there is any abnormal requirement for these tankers.  

### Power Futures and Spark Spread

- The spread between gas and power will have narrowed over the last week (gas has increased by ~2 basis points greater than power has across the full futures price curve), indicating that there is an increased squeeze for gas turbine profits, a lowering of the spark spread. If this sustains, it would be decreased incentive for these turbines to naturally operate within the power stack, increasing inter-day volatility in addition to the larger volatility introduced by the geopolitical and storage risks.
